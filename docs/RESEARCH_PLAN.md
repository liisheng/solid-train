## What the competition rewards

The official [GIBC V2 rules](https://gibc-v2.devpost.com/rules) require a model trained
from random initialization with at most 50,000,000 total trainable parameters, including
embeddings and output head. The scored evaluations are HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, PIQA,
WinoGrande, and held-out WikiText-103 perplexity. The
[overview](https://gibc-v2.devpost.com/) also scores reasoning, training efficiency,
innovation, and documentation/demo quality. Perplexity and accuracy are the first-listed
criterion and the first tie-breaker, so model quality should receive most of the effort.

## The essential reading list

### 1. Transformer and modern decoder architecture

- [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) -- the foundational
  Transformer paper. Read it to understand causal self-attention, residual blocks, and
  feed-forward layers.
- [LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)
  -- the practical modern decoder recipe: pre-normalization, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, rotary
  position embeddings, and efficient training on public data.

**Use here:** retain the current decoder-only Transformer with RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU,
causal attention, no unnecessary biases, and tied input/output embeddings. These are
proven, understandable choices that are easy to explain to judges.

### 2. Compute and token allocation

- [Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)
  -- the central paper for deciding how model size and training tokens interact.
- [Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264) -- useful
  when high-quality unique data is limited; supports controlled repetition of good data
  instead of filling the budget with poor text.

**Use here:** treat about 1B tokens as the first serious 50M checkpoint. Continue toward
2--3B only if held-out loss and benchmark likelihood continue improving. Track unique
tokens and source epochs; do not blindly repeat weak data.

### 3. Data quality--probably the largest available performance lever

- [The FineWeb Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17557) -- documents filtering and
  deduplication and introduces FineWeb-Edu, which improves knowledge and reasoning
  benchmarks including ARC.
- [DataComp-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11794) -- large controlled evidence that data
  filtering and curation can matter more than simply adding raw tokens.

**Use here:** make FineWeb-Edu the main corpus. Test a small amount of complementary
narrative text for HellaSwag/WinoGrande, science/reference material for ARC-Easy, and
procedural text for PIQA. Deduplicate and remove benchmark overlaps before tokenizer
training or model training.

### 4. Making tiny models useful

- [TinyStories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759) -- demonstrates that carefully
  capacity-matched language can produce coherent behavior in models far smaller than
  normal LLMs.

**Constraint lock:** TinyStories is informative research, but its text was generated by
GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. This project excludes commercial model-generated training data, so
TinyStories is not an eligible corpus component. If narrative data is tested later, it
must come from a documented non-synthetic public source.

### 5. Cheap, disciplined experiment selection

- [DataDecide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11393) (ICML 2025) -- strong open evidence that
  small proxy models and continuous likelihood metrics can guide pretraining-data choices
  without paying for many full-sized runs.

**Use here:** use the existing 12M pilot model to compare only a few meaningful data
mixtures at the same token count. Rank them using held-out loss and answer-option
log-likelihood margins, then confirm only the winner at 50M. This is the project's most
defensible innovation: research-guided mixture selection under consumer-GPU constraints.

### 6. Vocabulary under a strict parameter cap

- [Scaling Laws with Vocabulary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13623) (NeurIPS 2024) --
  includes models down to 33M and shows that vocabulary size is part of the model/compute
  tradeoff.

**Use here:** compare 8,192 and 16,384 byte-level BPE vocabularies while keeping total
parameters close to 50M. A larger vocabulary shortens sequences but consumes embedding
parameters; choose by actual pilot quality and throughput, not compression alone.

### 7. Correct evaluation

- [OLMES: A Standard for Language Model Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08446)
  (Findings of NAACL 2025) -- shows that prompt wording, task formulation, shots, and
  normalization can substantially change reported scores, especially for small base
  models.

**Use here:** freeze the exact `lm-evaluation-harness` version, task names, shot counts,
and metrics. Never tune a different prompt for each checkpoint. Publish the exact command
and raw result files.

## One optional architecture paper

- [MobileLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905) (ICML 2024) is the most relevant
  architecture paper for sub-billion models. It supports deep/thin designs, embedding
  sharing, and GQA. However, our local 24-layer GQA candidate was about 38% slower and
  0.23 validation-loss points worse than the conventional 12-layer model at the same
  early token count. Keep the paper as comparison and negative-result evidence; do not
  spend more compute on this direction unless later data overturns the result.

## Recommended final strategy

1. **Architecture:** keep the proven 12-layer, width-512, 8-head, SwiGLU/RMSNorm/RoPE
   model with tied embeddings, currently 49,295,872 parameters.
2. **Data:** FineWeb-Edu majority plus small, measured, non-synthetic narrative,
   science/reference, and procedural components. No scored benchmark examples or
   paraphrases and no commercial model-generated training text.
3. **Tokenizer:** run one controlled 8K-versus-16K BPE comparison; then freeze it.
4. **Ablations:** use the 12M proxy only for data mixture and tokenizer choice. Avoid a
   large architecture search.
5. **Training:** produce a fully evaluated checkpoint around 1B tokens; continue only if
   the curves justify it. Save checkpoints, metrics, speed, VRAM, elapsed time, and
   approximate FLOPs.
6. **Evaluation:** run the exact official harness on every finalist and WikiText-103 on
   the frozen holdout. Keep all settings identical.
7. **Innovation story:** emphasize capability-per-parameter, empirical mixture selection,
   contamination controls, and reproducibility on one consumer GPU.
8. **Submission:** allocate real time to the README, three clear screenshots, training
   curves, benchmark table, and a 2--5 minute working demo. These are explicitly judged,
   not decorative extras.

## Do not spend the hackathon on

- mixture-of-experts, state-space replacements, exotic attention, or long-context work;
- distillation, merging, pruning from pretrained models, or synthetic teacher imitation;
- RLHF/instruction tuning before the base-model benchmark is strong;
- dozens of tiny ablations with noisy accuracy results; or
- adopting a paper merely because it is newer than the validated foundations above.

The likely winning path is not the most complicated architecture. It is a well-trained,
cleanly evaluated modern Transformer with unusually disciplined data selection and a
convincing efficiency/reproducibility story.
